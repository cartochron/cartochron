Understand the Cartochron map

A guide to what you see, which controls to use, and what the observations can—and cannot—tell you.

Start here: what am I looking at?

A fix is one recorded location observation (latitude, longitude, and time). A track groups fixes from one recording run. Each track has an ID. Different IDs do not necessarily mean different people or devices.

A source is an operator-enrolled observation producer; a channel is an operator-configured acquisition input (for example, one GPSD UDP port). Neither name proves which physical device sent a report. A run is one recording session, with a separate ID. Historical runs are never joined merely because they share a channel or source.

The map joins nearby-in-time fixes into visible route segments, except across unusually large apparent receipt-time jumps (reviewed as uncertain, not automatically rejected). A line is a convenient way to read a sequence; it does not show every step taken between the recorded positions.

What does the time mean? The displayed time is when Cartochron's server received the observation, shown in your browser's local time zone. It may differ from the moment the GPS fix was obtained. The Metasploit adapter reads Android's cached last-known network-provider location, with unknown acquisition time and accuracy. GPSD may report a measurement timestamp and horizontal error estimate, neither independently verified by Cartochron.

1. Monitor a source or inspect a run

  1. In Monitor, choose a configured channel or enrolled source using Source / channel. That identity's newest run is the focused route. Separate markers show the latest reported positions of the other recently observed sources. Coincident reported coordinates share one symbol; click it to choose a source. No recordings are joined.
  2. Use Camera: Free leaves the map where you place it; Follow selected keeps the selected position within the inner map area; Follow visible keeps the visible markers in frame, including sources in different cities. Fit visible is a one-time action. Dragging or zooming manually turns following off. Show inactive positions reveals older reported positions, without claiming the phones are still present there.
  3. Recent receipts means Cartochron received an observation within two minutes, not that the device is connected. No recent receipts means the last observation is older; the route becomes muted. A source without channel metadata appears by its enrollment, while unattributed legacy recordings appear only as the newest unattributed run here—other runs remain in History.
  4. Switch to History and use Browse to filter by channel or enrolled source, or view all recordings. Older recordings loads the next page of runs. The run selector includes a human-readable name, recording period, observation count and exact run ID. Every run remains separate.
  5. Adjust From and To, then select Show interval. Dates are entered in your browser's local time zone.

Monitor and History share the map but remember their selections separately. Display names can be assigned by the receiver operator with the local channels.py CLI or changed with sources.py rename; doing so does not assign a source to a historical run or prove device identity. History pages through older recordings, but a paginated catalogue is not a frozen database snapshot.

The status text tells you how many fixes are shown. If you see Load more, additional observations are available; if you see interval complete, all observations in the requested interval have been loaded for the focused track. The viewer limits each displayed run to 5,000 fixes; narrow the interval when a result is marked partial.

Why did the map zoom very far out? An initial fit or Follow visible may need to show observations in distant cities, including inaccurate fixes. Choose Follow selected to focus one source, or Free to inspect without automated map movement. Monitor positions are not independent proof of physical presence.

2. Inspect individual points and missing time

In History, hover a small dot to see a fix's timestamp and coordinates. Click a dot or the route line to select a recorded point; the Inspect point slider moves with it. You can also move that slider yourself. The highlighted magenta point is your current selection.

When consecutive fixes are more than 10 minutes apart, Cartochron splits the line and puts a pale ring at each side of the gap. Select either ring to see the gap duration. The gap means no observations were recorded during that interval—not that the source stayed still or disappeared.

3. Put several tracks on the map

  1. In History, pick the focused recording using Recording run.
  2. Open Tracks and check up to three additional runs. The focused run stays blue; the others get contrasting outlined colors.
  3. For Comparison time, choose Entire runs (spatial) to see each run's loaded history even if they occurred at different times. Choose Same interval to limit the other runs to the focused run's From/To window.
  4. Click a colored dot or line to read the time and coordinates of a comparison fix.
A crossing is not a meeting. Two paths can intersect geographically while their observations were recorded hours apart. Even nearby fixes at nearly the same time do not establish identity, contact, or intent.

4. Ask: what was recorded at the same time?

In History, move Shared time at the bottom. It uses one time cursor across the currently loaded visible tracks. For each run, Cartochron highlights its latest actual fix at or before that time only if the fix is no more than five minutes old.

No recent fix means the viewer has no qualifying recorded fix near that instant. It does not mean the source was absent from the place. The cursor does not estimate movement between observations, and it cannot see pages that have not been loaded.

5. Ask: what was observed near this location?

  1. In History, open Inspect place.
  2. Choose a radius of 50 m, 100 m, or 250 m.
  3. Click the location on the map. A circle shows the selected area.
  4. Read the observations from visible, loaded runs in chronological order. Each result contains its time, track ID, and approximate distance from the selected location.

The loaded-points list displays at most 40 matching fixes and does not include unloaded pages or hidden tracks. For a separate search of all stored recording runs, click Search recorded history. The database results show the run ID, known source/channel labels, reported fix details and distance. Use Older search page until the search reports completion. An empty page can still have older candidates. Results are ordered by observation ingestion and the search is not a frozen snapshot when recording continues.

The database search accepts an optional Scope (exact channel or enrolled source) and local From/To controls. Those times filter the server receipt clock, not the GPS fix clock. Changing filters starts a new search, not a continuation of an earlier cursor. Above the individual fixes, Cartochron shows groups of matching observations from the retrieved pages only. Each group keeps one run and a maximum five-minute gap between matching receipt times; it is not a visit, dwell measurement or proof that a device remained inside the circle.

Candidate temporal proximity compares pairs of retrieved matching fixes from different runs within a chosen 1, 5 or 15 minute server-receipt window. The list shows each fix's ID, run, receipt time, their reported-coordinate separation, and any reported accuracy. Different enrolled source claims and different configured channel claims are labeled separately; neither is verified device identity. The same enrolled source or same channel is never counted as two identities. Channels with conflicting enrolled-source claims in retrieved results are excluded. Unattributed recordings cannot establish a comparable identity.

This counts observation pairs, not meetings or visits. High-frequency reports can yield many pairs; only 40 closest receipt-time pairs are displayed. Load older search pages or narrow the time range to test a hypothesis, and check for incomplete coverage. A missing pair does not establish that two devices were apart. The server's receipt clock is not necessarily the time a GPS fix was acquired.

Neither list establishes a continuous visit, physical identity or intent. A nearby fix may be cached or inaccurate; any stated accuracy comes from the producer, not from independent verification.

6. Compare two recorded runs without selecting a place

  1. In History, select the first recording run. Open Across places, then choose the second run from suggestions or enter its exact run ID if it is not listed.
  2. Choose a separation distance and a 1, 5 or 15 minute receipt-time window. The optional Use History From/To checkbox restricts the first run's anchor observations.
  3. Click Find observation pairs. The database searches across the two runs' recorded locations, not only the points loaded into the browser or near one selected place.
  4. Each result names two original fixes. Map pair frames the two reported positions as separate map markers, never as a joined route. Older candidate page advances the bounded comparison.

The comparison selects at most one nearby fix from the second run per examined fix in the first run. Pages examine up to 200 first-run observations and cap dense second-run candidate windows. The screen explicitly reports when further pages remain or candidate windows were capped. Results can differ if you reverse the selected runs. A missing pair is not proof of physical separation.

Runs sharing the same enrolled source or acquisition channel are not considered independent, and a channel with contradictory source assertions is refused. Different channel claims alone do not prove two phones. Matching receipt times do not establish matching GPS measurement times. These are observation witnesses, not meetings or visits.

7. Common questions

Why is my comparison track missing?

Open Tracks and verify that it is checked. If Comparison time says Same interval, its observations might fall outside the focused run's time window. Try Entire runs (spatial) to compare locations across different times.

Why is there no line, or why does the line stop?

A single fix has no route line. A line can stop at a recording gap of more than ten minutes, or at the edge of the selected interval or loaded pages. Look at the gap warning and the result status.

Can I conclude that two sources met?

No. You can inspect geographic proximity and compare receipt times, but Cartochron does not prove a meeting, a handoff, or continuous presence between observations. Treat those as hypotheses requiring independent evidence.

Why do the screenshots look different from my map?

The illustrated controls come from synthetic example tracks with an offline neutral background. Your browser normally shows OpenStreetMap tiles, which require network access. The map data underneath the routes may therefore look different.

Respect location privacy. Use Cartochron only with authorization. Recorded histories may disclose sensitive movements and must be protected and retained only as necessary. If you are unsure whether the displayed data is complete, check the time interval and partial-results messages before drawing conclusions.
